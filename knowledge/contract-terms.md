# Enterprise Contract Terms

## Term and Renewal

Enterprise contracts run for 12 months and renew automatically unless either
party gives 60 days' written notice. Renewal is at the then-current rate unless
the contract fixes a rate, which some multi-year agreements do.

Automatic renewal is a frequent source of a "we were charged after we intended to
cancel" report. Where the cancellation notice was given inside the 60-day window,
the contract has renewed and the charge is correct. Where notice was given outside
the window and the charge still occurred, that is a billing error and follows
[refund-policy.md](refund-policy.md).

## Termination

Termination for convenience requires the notice above and, if mid-term, the
early-termination fee specified in the contract — typically the remaining
committed value, capped at some multiple of the monthly charge depending on the
agreement.

Termination for cause — uncured material breach — has no fee, provided the breach
was notified and 30 days given to cure.

## Committed Spend

Some contracts carry a committed spend minimum. Where actual usage falls below
the commitment, the shortfall is invoiced at the end of the term. A customer
whose usage dropped mid-year and is invoiced for a shortfall is experiencing the
contract working as written; explain rather than credit.

## Price Protection

Contracts with price protection fix the rate for a defined period. Where the
protection has expired, an increase at renewal is permitted with 60 days' notice.

## Custom Refund Terms

Some Enterprise contracts contain refund terms that differ from the standard
policy — for example, a refund entitlement on an SLA breach. **Where the contract
specifies a remedy, the contract governs** and the standard policy's remedy does
not apply. The contract's own approval requirements, if any, also govern.

Determining whether a contract contains custom terms requires reading the
contract. An automated system does not have access to contract documents and must
therefore **escalate** any dispute where the customer asserts a contractual
entitlement, rather than applying the standard policy. Applying the standard
policy to a contract that overrides it produces a wrong answer with high
confidence, which is the worst available outcome.

## Assignment and Change of Control

Contracts may not be assigned without written consent. A change of control does
not void a contract, and a billing contact change following an acquisition is a
routine administrative update, not a new agreement.
