# Tax, VAT and Regional Billing

## Tax Determination

Tax is determined by the billing address on the account, not by the address of
the user who made the purchase. A change of billing address takes effect from the
next invoice; it does not re-rate an invoice already issued.

## VAT and GST

EU and UK accounts are charged VAT at the rate applicable to the billing country.
Reverse charge applies where a valid VAT number is on file; the invoice is then
issued without VAT and the customer accounts for it. A customer who supplies a
VAT number after an invoice was issued is not entitled to a re-issued invoice;
the correction applies from the next period.

Australian and New Zealand accounts are charged GST. Indian accounts are charged
GST at the applicable slab.

## Exemptions

Exempt and zero-rated supplies are applied only where a valid exemption
certificate is on file before the invoice is issued. A certificate supplied
afterwards does not trigger a refund of tax already remitted.

This is a genuine source of customer dissatisfaction and there is no remedy
available: once tax has been remitted to an authority, refunding it to the
customer would mean the business absorbs it. Explain the timing requirement
rather than escalating.

## Tax on Refunds

Where a refund is issued, the associated tax is refunded with it. The refund
amount communicated to the customer is the gross amount they were charged; the
tax portion is not netted off. A customer who was charged $129.00 including tax
receives $129.00 back.

## Invoices and Documentation

Invoices are issued electronically. A customer requiring a paper invoice or a
specific format for their jurisdiction should be routed to Finance Operations, not
resolved by Support.

## Currency and Exchange

Tax is calculated in the invoice currency. Where a customer's local currency
differs, the exchange rate applied is that of the invoice date, and the resulting
figure is what appears on the invoice.
